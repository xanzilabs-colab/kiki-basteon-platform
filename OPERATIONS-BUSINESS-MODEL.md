# Operations and Business Model (Sustainable, Institutional-First)

This document defines a practical, profitable, and sustainable model for Kiki × Basteon, with **institutional/organisational contracts as the core revenue engine**.

---

## 1) Strategic position

## Core thesis
- The most stable and scalable revenue comes from **B2B / B2G institutional contracts**.
- Kiki wearables should be treated mainly as a **distribution and adoption lever**, not the primary profit center.
- Consumer channels should improve market reach, trust, and lead generation into institutional contracts.

## Positioning statement
Kiki × Basteon is a safety operations platform that gives institutions:
- faster emergency response workflows,
- better duty-of-care evidence,
- safer communities/campuses/workforces,
- and measurable operational outcomes.

---

## 2) Revenue architecture (priority order)

## Revenue stream A — Institutional platform contracts (primary)
**Target customers:**
- universities and colleges
- schools and education groups
- residential estates and property portfolios
- logistics and field-work businesses
- healthcare and social-care organisations
- municipalities/public safety partnerships

**Commercial structure:**
- annual contract (preferred) or multi-year agreement
- base platform fee + usage bands
- optional premium modules and service levels

**Why this is primary:**
- predictable recurring revenue (ARR)
- stronger retention due to operational embedding
- lower churn than consumer-only subscriptions

## Revenue stream B — Implementation and onboarding services
- initial deployment fee
- data/config migration fee
- training and change-management packages
- branch/unit setup assistance

This creates near-term cash flow and funds customer success capacity.

## Revenue stream C — Support, SLA, and managed operations
- tiered support plans (business hours, extended, 24/7)
- incident response runbook support
- quarterly optimisation and governance reviews

## Revenue stream D — Analytics and governance add-ons
- executive reporting packs
- compliance/duty-of-care reports
- advanced responder performance and readiness dashboards

## Revenue stream E — Wearables and accessories (enablement channel)
- hardware sold or leased at modest margin
- bundles tied to platform contracts
- replacements/accessories/warranty upgrades

Important: keep wearable pricing competitive to maximize adoption and lock in platform usage.

## Revenue stream F — Consumer plans (secondary)
- freemium + premium personal safety plans
- buddy/trip/journal/calm upgrades
- guardian/family packs

Purpose: broaden user base, reduce CAC through referrals, and feed institutional pipeline.

---

## 3) Recommended pricing logic

Use a **hybrid pricing model** for institutions:

1. **Base tenant fee** (organisation-level license)  
2. **Active user/member bands** (beneficiaries, staff, responders)  
3. **Operational module add-ons** (advanced dispatch, analytics, integrations, SLA)  
4. **Services line items** (onboarding, training, policy tuning)  

This avoids underpricing large accounts while keeping smaller organisations accessible.

---

## 4) Wearables as distribution factor (not main profit center)

## Operating rule
- Treat wearables as an **on-ramp** into platform adoption.
- Price for accessibility and fleet rollout, not max hardware margin.

## Practical model
- include starter device packs in institutional onboarding
- offer subsidised hardware in exchange for minimum contract term
- provide “device + platform activation” bundles
- use device provisioning events to drive user onboarding completion

## Why this works
- faster activation
- stronger switching costs (workflow + devices + trained teams)
- more data continuity for response outcomes

## 5) Go-to-market strategy

## Segment priorities (first 24 months)
1. universities / campuses
2. estates / property groups
3. mid-sized enterprises with distributed staff
4. public-sector pilot corridors

## Entry offers
- 60–90 day pilot
- fixed pilot outcomes (response time, onboarding completion, readiness score)
- conversion pathway into annual contract with clear expansion pricing

## Expansion path inside each contract
- start with one branch/campus
- expand to multi-branch
- add responder modules + analytics + SLA tiers

---

## 6) Unit economics discipline

## Targets to manage
- gross margin by line:
  - platform SaaS: high margin target
  - services: healthy but lower margin
  - hardware: lower margin, strategic role
- CAC payback period per institutional customer
- net revenue retention (expansion minus churn)
- support cost per active organisation

## Financial principle
Use high-margin recurring software revenue to absorb:
- customer success,
- platform operations,
- and controlled hardware enablement discounts.

---

## 7) Operating model for sustainability

## Team lanes
- **Product & Engineering:** reliability, security, roadmap velocity
- **Customer Success:** onboarding, adoption, renewal readiness
- **Operations & Response Advisory:** deployment quality and responder workflows
- **Sales & Partnerships:** institutional pipeline and channel partners
- **Finance & RevOps:** pricing governance, margin control, forecast accuracy

## Governance cadence
- monthly: churn risk review + NRR review
- quarterly: pricing and packaging calibration
- biannual: segment profitability and roadmap ROI review

---

## 8) Contract design principles

- annual pre-paid options with discount incentives
- multi-year agreements for larger deployments
- minimum commitment thresholds for subsidised devices
- clear data governance and uptime/SLA clauses
- structured renewal with adoption KPIs

---

## 9) KPI framework (board and operator view)

## Growth KPIs
- ARR / MRR
- institutional pipeline value and conversion rate
- average contract value (ACV)
- expansion revenue %

## Adoption KPIs
- onboarding completion rates
- monthly active responders and operators
- linked devices per contracted user base

## Outcome KPIs
- median acknowledge time
- median dispatch time
- incident resolution cycle improvements
- responder availability coverage ratios

## Financial KPIs
- gross margin by stream
- CAC payback
- churn and retention (logo + revenue)
- runway and cash conversion efficiency

## 10) 12-month execution roadmap (commercial)

## Quarter 1
- finalise institutional packaging and pricing cards
- ship standard pilot-to-contract templates
- formalise customer success onboarding playbook

## Quarter 2
- launch partner channels (estate managers, campus integrators, security providers)
- add executive analytics packs for renewals and upsell
- implement SLA tiering and support automation

## Quarter 3
- expand to multi-site contracts and enterprise controls
- deepen organisation dashboard reporting value
- optimize device logistics and replacement workflow

## Quarter 4
- scale reference accounts into category case studies
- tighten renewal engine and expansion motion
- evaluate internationalisation or adjacent sectors

---

## 11) Risk controls

- avoid hardware-margin dependency
- avoid feature sprawl that increases support burden without revenue lift
- keep strict role-based data controls and auditability
- maintain strong uptime and incident communication standards

---

## 12) Bottom-line model summary

The sustainable model is:

1. **Institutional contracts as primary recurring revenue**  
2. **Wearables as a strategic adoption/distribution lever**  
3. **Services + SLA + analytics as margin-positive expansion layers**  
4. **Consumer channel as awareness + lead engine, not the main profit core**  

This structure is designed to be profitable, defensible, and self-sustaining while improving real-world safety outcomes.
